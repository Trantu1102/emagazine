Morning at the Huu Thanh Industrial Park in Duc Hoa commune, Tay Ninh province, begins with the steady rhythm of a factory where the line between people and technology has all but disappeared.

Across dozens of hectares, automated robots move pallets quietly through a smart warehouse system. Production lines run at speeds of up to thousands of cans a minute. From incoming raw materials to packaging, storage and logistics, every stage is linked by real-time data. Everything happens precisely, in step, with almost no room for error.

That is the first impression on entering the Suntory PepsiCo Tay Ninh Factory, a project with total investment of USD 300 million, covering 20 hectares, with a design capacity of more than 1.2 billion litres of product a year. It is not only the largest factory operated by Suntory PepsiCo Vietnam but also the largest production facility of either Suntory or PepsiCo anywhere in Asia-Pacific.

Seen from the province's perspective, the project's value lies in more than the scale of the investment or its production capacity. Speaking at the inauguration ceremony, Mr. Le Van Han, Deputy Secretary of the Provincial Party Committee and Chairman of the Tay Ninh Provincial People's Committee, described the Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory as a model of advanced technology and sustainable development. Its presence, he said, will help drive technology transfer, build a modern industrial ecosystem and create momentum for digital transformation, while raising the quality of the local workforce and improving the lives of local residents.

What sets this factory apart is the way the company has redefined what "modern manufacturing" means.

A shift towards a highly skilled workforce

A single canning line can now reach roughly 2,000 cans a minute, where earlier lines managed only 600 to 800. What stands out is that the number of operators has barely changed. Output has risen almost fourfold, but the workforce has shifted towards higher skills rather than higher headcount. The entire factory needs only around 300 technical staff to produce a volume that once required 400 to 500 workers.

According to Mr. Nguyen Thanh Quang, Head of Engineering at the Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory, the plant has been equipped with high-speed production lines, a smart warehouse and an automated energy management platform. Every stage, from quality inspection and materials intake through to finished-goods transport, is automated, streamlining processes while improving accuracy, operational safety and the efficient use of resources.

When technology becomes the answer to green growth

A modern factory should not only turn out products; it must become a place where technology, innovation and sustainable development are built into every operating process.

Prof. Dr. Nguyen Thi Thanh Mai
Member of the Party Central Committee, President of Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City

For decades, industrial growth has gone hand in hand with heavier energy use, greater extraction of resources and higher emissions. At the Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory, the question has been framed differently: how can a large-scale plant operate with a smaller environmental footprint?

The answer starts with the design. Sustainability was not an item added once the factory was finished; it was a principle running through everything from construction to daily operations. The plant holds LEED Gold green building certification, uses environmentally friendly materials, and integrates energy-saving and renewable energy solutions.

Most striking is that 100% of the steam used in production is generated from biomass fuel, mainly rice husks sourced locally. This does more than replace fossil fuels and cut greenhouse gas emissions: it puts agricultural by-products to use, adds value to the rice crop and builds a green supply chain linking the company with local farmers.

Together with rooftop solar, a smart energy management platform and a water recycling system, the factory optimises resource use at every stage. Treated water is reused for a range of internal activities; waste is sorted, with recycling or co-processing given priority; and more than 20% of the site is given over to trees and landscaping.

The plant is also ready to produce packaging made from 100% recycled plastic (rPET), laying the groundwork for a circular economy. In the beverage industry, this is not only a way to reduce virgin plastic but also to cut emissions across the entire value chain, moving towards a Net Zero target.

From energy to raw materials to packaging, every solution points in the same direction: use resources more efficiently and emit less. More broadly, it is Suntory PepsiCo's answer to the question of how to grow at a time when climate change is becoming a global challenge.

"When technology becomes the foundation of green growth, every product that leaves the line carries a commitment to sustainability with it."

Growth and the environment are no longer a trade-off. At the Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory, both goals are being realised within a single model of production.

Mr. Josuke Kimura, President and Chief Executive Officer of Suntory Beverage & Food, said the investment in the Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory not only affirms Suntory's long-term commitment to Vietnam but also strengthens the group's capacity to innovate and meet growing demand in the Vietnamese and regional markets.

If technology gives the factory its strength, it is people who represent the most lasting value Suntory PepsiCo is working towards. Mr. Ashish Joshi, Chief Executive Officer of Suntory PepsiCo Vietnam, stressed that the most successful investments are the ones that create value for the economy, for communities and for generations to come.

"The most successful investments are the ones that create value for the economy, for communities and for generations to come."

Mr. Pham Phu Ngoc Trai, former Chairman and Chief Executive Officer of PepsiCo Indochina, takes a similar view: the foundation of sustainable development lies not only in technology or the size of an investment, but in people, science, technology and innovation. Guided by that thinking, Suntory PepsiCo is not simply expanding production capacity but also investing in its workforce, strengthening the capabilities of domestic suppliers and helping Vietnamese businesses take a deeper role in the global value chain.

The project is expected to create around 3,000 jobs across the value chain. Beyond hiring, Suntory PepsiCo aims to deliver technical training, build skills and develop a highly qualified workforce capable of meeting the demands of modern manufacturing.

Looking further ahead, the company has chosen to partner with Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City to advance scientific research, technology transfer, innovation and workforce development for the years to come.

According to Prof. Dr. Nguyen Thi Thanh Mai, President of Vietnam National University Ho Chi Minh City, a modern factory can create more than products: it can set new standards for governance, technology, the environment, workplace culture and social responsibility, and become an important link in the national innovation ecosystem.

That philosophy also takes shape in community programmes Suntory PepsiCo has run for many years. The "Mizuiku – I Love Clean Water" programme has raised awareness of water conservation among more than 1.4 million students and over 21,000 teachers, while the "Water of Life" initiative restores watershed forests, conserves water resources and reduces carbon emissions.

There are also the bridges that connect communities, the clean water facilities, the support for children in difficult circumstances and the many local social welfare activities. For the provincial government, the project delivers more than economic value: it helps drive technology transfer, build a modern industrial ecosystem, raise the quality of the workforce and create momentum for the province's digital transformation.

That is why this factory is seen as something more than a manufacturing investment. It is a meeting point of science and technology, modern management, the green transition and social responsibility — a growth model in which economic performance goes hand in hand with environmental performance and value for the community.

Continuing the journey

"A factory can turn out billions of products a year. But lasting value is measured by what it leaves behind for the community and for the future."

Thirty years ago, PepsiCo came to Vietnam wanting to open a new market. Today, Suntory PepsiCo has chosen Vietnam for its largest investment anywhere, with the aim of building a new model of manufacturing for the region.

From the high-speed production lines, the data-driven robots and the renewable energy systems through to the water conservation programmes, the workforce development and the work alongside local communities, one philosophy runs through all of it: growth only truly matters when it delivers lasting value for people and for nature.

"Growth only truly matters when it delivers lasting value for people and for nature."

Mr. Eugene Willemsen, Chief Executive Officer of International Beverages and Executive Vice President of PepsiCo, described Vietnam as one of PepsiCo's most dynamic growth markets. Investing in the Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory, he said, not only raises production capacity but also affirms the company's commitment to Vietnam's goal of growth built on innovation and sustainability.

The Suntory PepsiCo Long An Factory is therefore more than the "heart" of a manufacturing network in Asia. It is a symbol of a new approach to modern industry, one where technology serves people, innovation goes hand in hand with green development, and every investment made today is measured by the value it leaves for generations to come.

More than USD 300 million is the scale of the project. But what this investment is reaching for is not only volume or market share: it is confidence in Vietnam's ability to master technology, drive innovation and build lasting value for future generations.
